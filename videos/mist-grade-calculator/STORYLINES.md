# Storylines — MIST Grade Calculator

Three different films, not three titles on one film. Each has its own world, device and form.

---

## 1. THE NUMBER LANDS  *(recommended)*

**Device:** a number lands — one real number earned by a scene, then made physical.
**Form:** one continuous take. The camera never cuts; it pushes into the sheet, travels
down it, and pulls back to the campus.

**What the viewer sees and feels:** The film opens inside the site's own sheet, already
mid-life: the ink verdict band with 240 struck across it. A cursor enters from off-frame
and fills the six fields one by one, left to right, and the "Achieved so far" number in
the volt cell climbs from 0.00 to 114.00. The verdict band answers — 126 — and the number
is huge, occupying the frame. The camera pulls back to the whole sheet, then travels down
the ruled page: the six-marks table, the two course weightings, the grade ladder with its
one lit cell, and finally the campus photographs at dusk. The last thing is the mark and
the address.

**First two seconds, exactly:** Full-bleed the site's ink verdict band. "240" in condensed
caps at 18% of the frame height. A hairline rule draws itself across the frame. Cut of
the cursor arrow entering from the bottom-right, growing from 0 to 4% of frame width.

**Beats:** hook (the number is already there) → the sheet being filled → the answer changes
→ the rules of the semester → the ladder → the campus → the mark and the address.

**Look:** The site's own Sport theme — cool concrete paper (oklch 98% .005 250), blue-black
ink (oklch 16% .08 260), volt accent (oklch 88% .2 120) used only as a fill on one cell and
one number per frame. Saira Condensed 800 for every figure and title, Albert Sans for
body, JetBrains Mono for the tabular numbers. Hairlines at 1px, bar rules at 2px. The
campus photographs in the site's own duotone — grayscale 1, contrast 1.2, multiplied into
the ink. No gradients, no glow, no glass, no particles.

**Sound:** One music bed, 30 s, Python-generated MusicGen. Focused pulse, no melody in the
first 3 s, a clean entry on the beat the verdict band answers. A soft click per field
filled. A low settle when the answer lands.

**Why this one:** it uses the captured site as itself — the real sheet, the real numbers,
the real photographs — and the answer to the question is the thing the whole film is about.
No invented screen, no mockup, nothing a user could find and call false.

## 2. THE LAST ROW

**Device:** the object is the explanation — a sheet whose last line is the answer.
**Form:** object-to-object chain. Every shot ends on the thing that opens the next: the
verdict band becomes the table's Final row, the table's Final row becomes the ladder's
F* cell, the ladder becomes the campus tower.

**What the viewer sees and feels:** A ledger sheet on paper, seen flat from above. The
camera tracks right across ruled rows — Class tests, Mid term, Attendance, Performance —
and lands on the last row, Final, and the row is empty. Everything else on the sheet fills
in around it; the Final row stays blank and the frame holds on the gap. The title: "The
Final is what is left." Then the gap fills with a number, and the sheet resolves into the
real site. Cuts are matches: a ruled line becomes a ladder step becomes a tower edge.

**First two seconds, exactly:** A blank ruled sheet fills the frame, 12 horizontal rules
in ink on paper, seen flat. The top rule is thicker. Nothing else. The cursor's shadow
crosses the frame before the cursor does.

**Beats:** hook (a sheet with one blank row) → the rules of the semester → the blank row →
the answer → the ladder → the campus → the mark.

**Look:** Identical palette to #1, but the camera is orthographic and flat — no perspective,
no depth. Rule weight is the only hierarchy: 2px for the sheet's edges, 1px for the rows,
4px for the row that matters. Everything is type and rules on paper.

**Sound:** Same music bed, more percussive, entered on the first rule.

## 3. THE HOUR THE SEMESTER ENDS

**Device:** a moment in someone's day — the tower at night, the last exam behind you.
**Form:** a flight through one world — a night campus the camera travels across, with the
sheet as one stop on the route.

**What the viewer sees and feels:** The site's own duotone campus photograph, full-bleed and
still for a beat — Tower 4 after the light goes. Then the camera moves across it and the
sheet rises out of the dark: the real interface, at 2x, filling and filling. The film's
emotional argument is the site's own last line: "The sheet above is the part you can still
change." The photograph and the calculator are the same night.

**First two seconds, exactly:** The night photograph, full-bleed, duotone, dark. One lit
window moves as the camera drifts. The word "Final" in condensed caps fades up in paper
white over the image at 6% of the frame height.

**Beats:** hook (the night campus) → the sheet wakes in the dark → the fields fill → the
answer → the ladder lights up → the wide shot again, now with the address.

**Look:** The duotone at full strength. Ink blue-black at 12% lightness over the
photographs, paper white for all type. The volt accent is the only colour in the film and
appears only on the achieved number and the A+ cell.

**Sound:** Music bed under, with a wide, airy reverb — a night exterior — and the field
clicks dry and close, as if in a quiet room.

**Why not first:** it is beautiful and on-brand, but it opens on a photograph rather than
on the product, and the product is the whole reason the film exists. It is the better
film for a brand piece and the weaker launch film.
